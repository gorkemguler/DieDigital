# 7. Real-world traces

The most dangerous information online is what makes you physically findable: your home, your work, your routine, your car, your face. This chapter closes the places where digital data meets the physical world.

## 7.1 Your address

Your address may be written in all of these places; check each one:

- **Online shopping, delivery, grocery and parcel apps:** Delete saved addresses (before deleting the account, and also if you're keeping it).
- **Listing sites:** Old rental or sale listings for your home can show the inside of your flat, the door number, the street and your phone number. If you didn't post the listing (landlord, agent), ask them to take it down.
- **Domain registration (WHOIS):** If you own a domain, turn on the privacy service ([chapter 9](09-final-steps.md)).
- **Company registers:** If you're a director or shareholder, your address may be on the public company register. Many registers let you use a service or business address instead of your home address; ask your accountant or the registry.
- **Public notices:** Insolvency, enforcement, auction and service-by-publication notices are often published with names and sometimes addresses. They can't be removed; they drop off when their publication period ends.
- **Electoral registers:** In the UK you can ask your council to remove you from the *open* register (the version that can be sold). In many US states voter records are public; some states offer confidentiality for specific groups.
- **Property records:** In many places, property ownership records are public. Some jurisdictions allow certain people (for example abuse survivors, judges, police officers) to shield their address.
- **Phone directories:** If you have a landline, ask your provider whether your name and address are listed in directories, and opt out.
- **Old websites and CVs:** Putting your home address on a CV used to be common; run the document searches in [chapter 4](04-search-engines-and-archives.md).

**If you're moving:** Give your new address only where it's required (government, bank, employer, essential utilities). For parcels, use pickup points, lockers or collection from the depot ([chapter 11](11-living-invisibly.md)).

## 7.2 Street-level imagery

You can ask for your home, car or face to be blurred in street-level imagery. Blurring is **permanent** and can't be undone.

- **Google Street View:** Open the image > "Report a problem" at the bottom right > choose face, home, car or licence plate > mark the area to blur.
- **Apple Maps (Look Around):** Apple accepts blurring requests by email (MapsImageCollection@apple.com).
- **Yandex Panoramas:** The "Report a problem" option on the panorama.

Check older captures too; Street View's timeline can show earlier imagery.

## 7.3 Location data

Location reveals your routine: home, work, gym, your children's school.

- **Google Maps Timeline:** Turn it off and delete the history (Maps > profile > Your Timeline).
- **Apple Significant Locations:** Settings > Privacy & Security > Location Services > System Services > Significant Locations: turn off and clear history.
- **Location sharing:** Google Maps location sharing, Apple Find My > People, WhatsApp live location, Life360 and family tracking apps. Review who you share with.
- **Snapchat Snap Map:** Turn on Ghost Mode.
- **Fitness and running apps:** Public activities on apps like Strava reveal your home. Set activities to "Only you", create privacy zones around home and work, and opt out of the aggregated heatmap.
- **Location in photos (EXIF):** Turn off location tagging in your camera settings. Remove location data from old photos before sharing (iOS: when sharing, "Options > Location"; on Android, the details menu of your gallery app).
- **App permissions:** Give location only to apps that really need it, "while using the app" only, with precise location off.

## 7.4 Home network and Wi-Fi

Phones report nearby Wi-Fi networks to location services, so your home router sits in those databases as a location, and that can reveal whose home you're in.

- **Add `_nomap` to the end of your network name** (for example `HomeNet_nomap`). Google, Apple and Microsoft say they exclude networks with this suffix from their location databases.
- Don't use your name or flat number in the network name.
- When you move, replace the router or rename the network; that breaks the link with your old address.

## 7.5 Smart devices and voice assistants

- **Voice assistant recordings:** Google Assistant (Google My Activity > Web & App Activity, including audio recordings), Alexa (Alexa app > Privacy > Review voice history), Siri (Settings > Siri > Siri & Dictation History > Delete). Delete them and turn off recording.
- **Smart and doorbell cameras:** Delete cloud recordings and shared access. Your neighbours' cameras may record you too; there's nothing you can do about those.
- **Smart TVs:** Turn off automatic content recognition (ACR), which collects your viewing habits.
- **Robot vacuums, smart plugs, thermostats:** These know your floor plan and your routine; delete the app accounts.
- **Car apps:** Connected-car apps keep location history. Delete the account; when selling the car, factory-reset the infotainment system and delete paired phones and saved addresses.

## 7.6 Health data

- **Health portals and patient apps:** Review sharing and privacy settings; check which data is shared with whom.
- **Health and fitness apps:** Period and pregnancy tracking, sleep, nutrition, heart rate and mental health apps hold special-category data. Make a separate deletion request before deleting the account.
- **Smartwatches and fitness bands:** Delete the linked app account; reset the device.
- **Online consultation and appointment sites:** Doctor reviews and Q&A history may appear under your name.

## 7.7 DNA tests

If you've taken a DNA test, your genetic data identifies not only you but your relatives, and it can never be changed.

1. If you want the raw data, download it first.
2. In the account settings, request **deletion of your genetic data** and **destruction of your sample** (these may be separate options).
3. Withdraw consent for research.
4. Turn off relative matching, then delete the account.

DNA companies can be sold or go bankrupt, and the data passes to the new owner. Don't wait on the assumption that it's safe where it is.

## 7.8 Car and licence plate

- If your plate is visible in a used-car listing, remove the listing or have it removed.
- Social media photos where your plate is visible.
- Plate blurring in Street View (7.2).
- Accounts in parking, toll and congestion-charge apps.

## 7.9 What your photos show

Before deciding which photos to keep, look at them again with this in mind: the view from your window, your building entrance, the door number, a school uniform, a work badge, an open document on a screen, reflections in mirrors, medicine boxes, boarding passes. Any one of these can reveal your address or routine.
