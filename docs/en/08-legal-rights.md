# 8. Legal rights: GDPR, US state laws, KVKK

> This chapter is general information, not legal advice. In a serious dispute, talk to a lawyer.

Against companies that offer no delete option, say "we can't delete that", or don't reply at all, your strongest tool is your legal right to deletion. Once you send a formal request, the company has a legal deadline and a recorded obligation. Use this chapter alongside chapters 3, 5, 6 and 7.

## 8.1 Which law applies to you?

| Where you live / where the company is | Law | Deletion right | Deadline | Complaints to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EU / EEA | GDPR | Yes (art. 17) | 1 month, extendable by 2 | Your national data protection authority |
| United Kingdom | UK GDPR and Data Protection Act 2018 | Yes | 1 month, extendable by 2 | ICO (https://ico.org.uk) |
| California | CCPA as amended by CPRA | Yes | 45 days, extendable by 45 | California Privacy Protection Agency (https://cppa.ca.gov), state Attorney General |
| Other US states | State comprehensive privacy laws (more than a dozen states) | Usually yes | Usually 45 days | State Attorney General |
| Turkey | KVKK (Law No. 6698) | Yes (art. 7, 11) | 30 days | Personal Data Protection Board |
| Elsewhere | Check your country's data protection law | Often yes | Varies | Your data protection authority |

Two practical points:

- **The law of the company matters too.** A company established in the EU, or targeting people in the EU, is bound by the GDPR. Many global companies run one privacy process worldwide and handle deletion requests the same way wherever you live, so a well-written request often works even where the law doesn't strictly require it.
- **Big platforms use forms.** Google, Meta, Microsoft, Apple and Amazon generally route requests through privacy forms rather than email. Search their help pages for "privacy request", "data subject request" or "GDPR". If there's no form, email the data protection officer (DPO) or privacy contact in the privacy policy.

## 8.2 GDPR and UK GDPR

| Article | Right |
|---|---|
| 15 | Access: what data they hold, where it came from, who it was shared with |
| 16 | Rectification |
| **17** | **Erasure ("right to be forgotten")** |
| 18 | Restriction of processing |
| 19 | Notifying recipients of erasure or rectification |
| 20 | Data portability |
| 21 | Objection; objection to direct marketing is absolute |
| 12(3) | Response deadline: 1 month (+2 months with justification) |
| 12(5) | Free of charge |
| 12(6) | Identity verification: only what's reasonable |
| 77 | Complaint to a supervisory authority |

Exceptions to erasure (art. 17(3)): freedom of expression and information, a legal obligation, public interest, and establishing or defending legal claims.

You can complain to the authority in the country where you live, where you work, or where the infringement happened. The European Data Protection Board's website lists every national authority.

## 8.3 United States

There's no single federal privacy law with a general right to delete. Instead:

- **California (CCPA/CPRA):** Covered businesses must, on request, tell you what personal information they hold, delete it, correct it, and stop selling or sharing it. They have 45 days to respond (extendable once by another 45 days with notice) and must verify your identity. Data brokers must register with the state, and California's DROP platform lets residents send one deletion request to all registered brokers.
- **Other states:** Virginia, Colorado, Connecticut, Texas, Oregon and a growing list of others have similar rights to access, delete and opt out of targeted advertising and sale. Thresholds and exemptions vary; most give companies 45 days.
- **Global Privacy Control:** A browser setting that signals "do not sell or share my data". California and several other states require businesses to honour it ([chapter 11](11-living-invisibly.md)).
- **If you're not covered:** Ask anyway. Many companies apply their California process nationwide.

## 8.4 Turkey (KVKK)

Law No. 6698 on the Protection of Personal Data.

- **Rights (art. 11):** to learn whether your data is processed and why, who it was transferred to, to have it corrected, **erased or destroyed** (under art. 7), to have recipients notified, to object to automated decisions, and to compensation.
- **Art. 7:** Data must be erased on request once the conditions for processing no longer apply; legally required retention must be explained, with its period.
- **Constitution, art. 20(3):** everyone has the right to request protection of their personal data, including its correction or deletion. The right to be forgotten has also been recognised by the Constitutional Court (application N.B.B., 2013/5653, 3 March 2016) and the Court of Cassation's General Assembly of Civil Chambers (E. 2014/4-56, K. 2015/1679).
- **How to apply** (Communiqué on the Procedures and Principles of Application to the Data Controller): in writing (wet-signed letter by hand, registered mail or notary), by **registered electronic mail (KEP)**, with a secure electronic or mobile signature, **from the email address you previously gave the company and that is registered in its systems**, or through a form the company provides. The request should contain your name, (if written) signature, national ID number, address for service, contact details and the subject of the request. Whether to include your ID number in a first email request is a judgement call; many companies identify you by your registered email.
- **Where to send it:** the company's *Aydınlatma Metni* (privacy notice), or look the company up on VERBİS (https://verbis.kvkk.gov.tr).
- **Deadlines:** the company must respond within **30 days** (art. 13). If it refuses, answers inadequately or doesn't answer, you can complain to the Personal Data Protection Board within 30 days of the answer and in any case within **60 days** of your application (art. 14). You must apply to the company first.

The Turkish templates (KVKK request, reminder, Board complaint) are in [the Turkish appendix](../ek-b-dilekce-ornekleri.md).

## 8.5 Step by step: one deletion request

1. **Find the right contact:** privacy policy, DPO address, privacy form, or the national register of data controllers where one exists.
2. **Prepare the request:** copy the right template from [Appendix B](appendix-b-letter-templates.md) and fill in the brackets. Include everything that identifies the account: registered email, username, phone, profile URL.
3. **Send it:** from the email address registered with the account, or through the company's form. Keep a copy (Bcc yourself, or keep the sent item); this is your proof.
4. **Record it:** date sent, the deadline, and the date by which you'd have to complain.
5. **Keep the acknowledgement:** many companies give you a reference number.
6. **At the deadline:** if there's no answer, send a reminder ([Appendix B](appendix-b-letter-templates.md)).
7. **Still nothing:** complain to the authority.

**Tip:** For companies when you don't know what they hold (data brokers, ad companies, lookup apps), the template also asks what they hold and where they got it. The answer can lead you to the original source, which you can then deal with too.

## 8.6 Content about you online

If a website publishes content about you that is defamatory or invades your privacy, the route depends on where you are: data protection requests to the site and to search engines ([chapter 4](04-search-engines-and-archives.md)), defamation or privacy claims, and in some countries fast-track removal procedures (Turkey's Law No. 5651, art. 9 and 9/A, for example). If you're being harassed or stalked: [chapter 12](12-stalking-harassment-abuse.md).

## 8.7 When they reply

- **"Deleted":** Keep the confirmation and mark it in your list.
- **"We need to verify your identity":** Give only what's proportionate (see the identity verification trap in [chapter 0](00-getting-started.md)).
- **"We have no account / data for you":** You may be registered under another email or phone number; ask again with all your identifiers.
- **"Some data will be kept for legal reasons":** Ask in writing which data, on what legal basis and for how long. It must not be used for anything else in the meantime; ask them to confirm marketing has stopped.
- **"You can delete your account yourself":** Do so, but also ask for data outside the account (marketing lists, analytics, backups) to be deleted.
- **Refusal or silence:** Send a reminder; if that fails, complain within the deadlines.

## 8.8 Complaining

Include in your complaint:

- The text and date of your original request (with proof it was sent)
- The company's reply, if any, and why it's inadequate
- Your reminder and its date
- What you want: deletion of your data, and a proper answer to your request
