# 13. Monitoring and maintenance

Deleting is not a one-off job. Data brokers rebuild profiles, old pages get reindexed, a forgotten account surfaces in a breach, a friend posts a new photo. This chapter keeps you where you've disappeared to.

## Always on

- **Google Alerts** (https://www.google.com/alerts): for your name (in quotes, with spelling variants), email, phone and usernames. Send the alerts to an email alias.
- **Google "Results about you" alerts** (if available in your region).
- **Have I Been Pwned notifications** (https://haveibeenpwned.com/NotifyMe): for every email address you have, so you hear about new breaches and can change passwords.

## Every month

- Confirm the account deletions whose waiting period has ended ([chapter 3](03-deleting-accounts.md)).
- Send reminders on requests past their deadline; decide on complaints for those running out of time ([chapter 8](08-legal-rights.md)).

## Every three months

- Repeat the searches from [chapter 4](04-search-engines-and-archives.md) on four search engines and in reverse image search. For each new result, get the source removed, then ask the search engine to refresh.
- Check your usernames on the big sites again: someone may have taken an old username and be impersonating you.
- Check the data broker sites again; you may have been relisted ([chapter 5](05-data-brokers-and-lookup-apps.md)).
- Look for new tags and photos in friends' and family's posts.

## Every year

- Review the privacy settings of every account you keep; platforms change settings and defaults.
- Update your inventory: new accounts you opened during the year, ones you closed.
- Review the apps on your phone and their permissions.
- Check Street View for new imagery of your home; a new capture may need blurring again.
- If you set up a dead man's switch, check that the plan is still what you want (below).

> **With the tool:** `diedigital footprint dorks`, `footprint username`, `footprint email` and `inventory overdue` cover most of these checks in one go ([Appendix C](appendix-c-tools.md)).

## If something comes back

1. **Don't panic; record it:** link, date, screenshot.
2. **Find the source:** Who published it? An old account of yours, someone else's post, a data broker, an archive?
3. **Go back to the relevant chapter:** your own content, chapters 2-3; someone else's, 6; a search result, 4; a broker, 5; the legal route, 8.
4. **Dry up the source:** Why did the same information come back? There may still be an open account, a mutual friend, a loyalty card or an app permission behind it.

## Maintaining your dead man's switch

If you set one up ([chapter 10](10-dead-mans-switch.md)):

- Don't miss the check-in reminder in your calendar.
- If a preflight warning arrives (expired token, changed password), fix it straight away.
- If you open a new account or find a new privacy contact, update your list (and `accounts.json` if you use the tool).
- If you use scheduled emails, check that the recipient addresses are still valid.
- Update the instructions you left with your digital executor once a year.
- If you use the tool, review the plan with a `dry-run` once a year.

## When is it "done"?

It's never completely done, but at this point you've disappeared:

- Searches for your name, email, phone number and usernames return nothing that belongs to you,
- Every account in your inventory is either deleted or kept deliberately because it's unavoidable,
- You have a recorded legal request for every place that couldn't be deleted,
- You're not creating new traces.

From here on, it's a half-hour check every three months.
