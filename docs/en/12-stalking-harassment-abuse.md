# 12. Stalking, harassment, abuse: if someone is looking for you

> **If you are in immediate danger,** call your local emergency number: 112 (EU, Turkey and many other countries), 911 (US, Canada), 999 (UK).
> **Helplines:** US National Domestic Violence Hotline 1-800-799-7233 · UK National Domestic Abuse Helpline 0808 2000 247 · Turkey ALO 183 (and the KADES app for women). Elsewhere, search for your country's domestic violence or victim support helpline.

If you're hiding from a specific person (an ex-partner, a family member, a stalker, a harasser), read this chapter **before** the others. In this situation the order of disappearing is different, because:

- Suddenly deleting everything can **alert** the other person and make things escalate.
- Deleting can destroy **evidence** you'll need later.
- Devices and accounts the other person once had access to can still be an **open door**.

If you can, don't do this alone: plan it with a lawyer, a legal aid service, a domestic violence or victim support organisation, or the police.

## 12.1 Evidence first

Before you delete anything:

- **Screenshot messages, posts and comments:** with the sender's name/username, the date and time, and (on the web) the URL in the address bar visible.
- **Record profile pages** and fake accounts: profile URL, user ID.
- **Voice messages, calls, emails:** Keep them, emails with full headers ("show original").
- **Formal preservation:** For important content, a notary, bailiff or a certified web-capture service (depending on your country) can record web pages in a form that carries more weight in court than a screenshot.
- **Keep a dated incident log:** what happened, when, where, who witnessed it.
- Store the evidence encrypted, somewhere the other person can't reach, in two copies (the backup method in [chapter 1](01-inventory-and-backup.md)).

## 12.2 Then safety: close the doors

Do this from a **clean device** (a phone or computer the other person has never had access to):

1. **Check your devices:**
   - Unknown apps, apps with "device admin" rights (Android: Settings > Security > Device admin apps), unknown profiles (iOS: Settings > General > VPN & Device Management).
   - **Safety Check on iPhone** (Settings > Privacy & Security > Safety Check): shows on one screen what you share with whom, and can stop all of it at once.
   - If you suspect stalkerware: https://stopstalkerware.org. Resetting a suspect device straight away can show the other person you've noticed and wipe the evidence; talk to a specialist first.
2. **Turn off location sharing:** Google Maps, Apple Find My, WhatsApp live location, Snap Map, Life360, family tracking apps, car apps.
3. **Check for trackers:** iPhones warn about unknown AirTags automatically; on Android, turn on "Unknown tracker alerts" and run a scan. Check your car, bag and coat.
4. **Change every password,** starting with email, then your mobile carrier account, cloud, bank and social media. Don't use anything the other person could guess (birthdays, shared memories).
5. **Log out other sessions:** In every account, check "log out of all devices" / the sessions list and remove devices you don't recognise.
6. **Change recovery details:** recovery email, recovery phone, security questions. The other person's email or phone may still be set as a recovery option.
7. **Turn on two-factor authentication,** with an authenticator app rather than SMS.
8. **Leave shared accounts:** family plans (Apple Family Sharing, Google Family), shared cloud albums, shared calendars, shared Netflix/Spotify, smart home apps (the other person may still have access to your doorbell camera or thermostat).
9. **Bank and mobile carrier:** Ask your carrier for extra protection (a PIN or password) against number porting and SIM swaps; set up extra verification at your bank.

## 12.3 Protect your address

- Don't share your new address with anyone; use pickup points for parcels ([chapter 11](11-living-invisibly.md)).
- Don't tell mutual acquaintances your new address, workplace or routine; even someone well-meaning can pass it on without realising.
- Apply every address and location step in [chapter 7](07-real-world-traces.md), including Street View blurring.
- **Address confidentiality:** Many US states run Address Confidentiality Programs that give survivors a substitute address for public records. In the UK you can register to vote anonymously if your safety is at risk. In Turkey, protective measures under Law No. 6284 (on the Protection of the Family and Prevention of Violence against Women) can include restraining orders, a ban on contact, keeping your identity and address confidential and, where there's a risk to life, changing your identity details; see the [Turkish chapter](../12-takip-taciz-siddet.md). Ask local support services what exists where you live.
- **Stalking is a crime** in most countries (in Turkey, Turkish Criminal Code art. 123/A). Protective and restraining orders are usually available through the police or the courts.

## 12.4 Then deletion: the safe order

1. **You have the evidence, and you've closed off devices and accounts.**
2. **Quiet changes first:** Make accounts private; quietly restrict the other person and mutual acquaintances. Sudden blocking and mass deletion get noticed; decide according to your safety plan.
3. **Location and address traces** ([chapter 7](07-real-world-traces.md)): the most critical ones.
4. **Photos and posts that reveal your routine** ([chapter 2](02-content-cleanup.md)).
5. **What mutual friends have posted** ([chapter 6](06-traces-held-by-others.md)): ask only people you trust.
6. **Deleting accounts and everything else,** in the guide's normal order.
7. **New number, new email, new accounts** ([chapter 11](11-living-invisibly.md)): with no link to anything the other person knows.

## 12.5 Impersonation and exposure

- **Fake accounts:** Platforms have impersonation report forms; you may need to prove your identity. Record the fake account first.
- **Non-consensual intimate images:** Google's personal information removal form covers this ([chapter 4](04-search-engines-and-archives.md)), and most platforms have a priority reporting route. StopNCII (https://stopncii.org) creates a fingerprint on your device so participating platforms can block re-uploads; the image never leaves your device. Report it to the police too; in many countries this is a crime.
- **Doxxing:** If your address, phone number or ID details have been posted: record the evidence, report it to the platform, ask Google to remove the results, and report it to the police.
